Modern CSS has superpowers: real trigonometry, exponents, custom functions, even conditionals via `if()`. Two things it famously lacks: loops, and variables you can update. A training loop needs both.

So I built one anyway - [a tiny neural network that trains itself, live, in pure CSS](https://acorns.nutlore.me/css-neural-network/). No JavaScript on the page. None in a build step either.

The trick, in three lines:

- **Variables live in scrollbars.** Scroll position is the one number the browser keeps between frames that a stylesheet can both read and write. Each parameter is a scroller - how far it is scrolled *is* the value.
- **Reads and writes are layout tricks.** A `scroll-timeline` reads the position back as a number. `calc()` computes real residuals and gradients from the training data - the dataset sits in the stylesheet as variables, and the labels come from asking `sin()` at runtime. Then `scroll-snap-type: mandatory` writes the result: park the snap target somewhere, and the scrollbar must follow. That drag is the assignment.
- **The render loop is the `for` loop.** Nothing ticks the machine. Being rendered is being ticked - one momentum step per frame, forever.

The story on the page: a goldfish 🐠 learns to swim like `sin(x)`, taught by a fish that knows the answer natively. You can grab a scrollbar mid-training and fight the optimizer with your thumb. The optimizer wins. It has more stamina.

Needs a recent Chrome ([animation-timeline](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/CSS/Reference/Properties/animation-timeline), [@function](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/CSS/Reference/At-rules/@function), [sibling-index()](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/CSS/Reference/Values/sibling-index)). Elsewhere the tank freezes over for the season - the teacher swims on under the ice.

The full write-up lives on the demo page itself: the two-phase flip-flop that dodges the self-reference guard, the math in MathML, and the optimizer in CSS, piece by piece. [Go poke the fish.](https://acorns.nutlore.me/css-neural-network/)
