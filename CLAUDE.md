# entre

Personal blog. Posts live in `blog/` as MDX files.

## Writing style for blog posts

When drafting or editing prose in `blog/`, follow these rules. The audience includes non-native English speakers; complex words, passives, and dense constructions slow them down without adding value.

### Voice and shape

- **Casual register, not academic.** Match the voice of existing posts: hedging ("Suggests, not proves."), parenthetical asides, occasional emoji (😉 sparingly), short claim-sized sentences, regular dashes (`-` with spaces, not em-dashes).
- **Simple words over complex ones, without losing context.** Prefer "model" over "apparatus", "push" over "drive", "leaves out" over "does not yet say". When in doubt, pick the word a non-native reader will recognise faster. Strip register, not nuance.
- **Avoid passive voice when possible.** Prefer "Plenty of tools cover this" over "The category is well-served". Prefer "I built my own" over "My own was built". Static `was/is + infinitive` ("was to use one") often reads passive-adjacent and can usually be flipped active.
- **Rhythm and whitespace matter.** Short claim-sized sentences. One idea per sentence where possible. Break long paragraphs. Bullet lists for parameter-style enumerations. Variation in length over wall-of-text.
- **Simple constructions, and make the ideas flow.** Beyond simple *words*, prefer simple *sentence shapes*: avoid nested clauses, long pre-modifiers, and passive frames when an active one fits. Each sentence should hand off cleanly to the next - when a paragraph reads as a list of disconnected claims, the transitions are missing. Read aloud; if you trip, restructure.
- **Cut redundant possessives and repeats.** Prefer *"your `a`, `v`, `c`"* over *"your `a`, your `v`, your `c`"*. Once the framing is set in the first item, do not re-establish it in each one that follows.

### Editing habits

- **Cut the scaffolding.** Drop throat-clearing transitions like *"There is one assumption underneath all of this, though, and it is worth naming"* - just state the assumption. Same for *"Which raises the next question"* - just ask the question.
- **Share, don't praise.** Frame insights as properties of the work (*"the recipe leans on familiarity"*), not author achievements (*"as a fullstack developer I have built this many times over"*). Generalise the lesson to the reader rather than narrating personal experience.
- **Concrete callbacks over vague claims.** When stating something abstract, pull specific moments from earlier in the post so the abstract point lands as a verifiable claim, not vibes.
- **Teasers commit to specifics.** Not *"more to come"* - state what the next post will actually do.
- **Don't invent rationale.** When explaining a choice (why a technology was picked, why a decision was made), ask rather than guess plausible-sounding reasons. Plausible ≠ true.

### How to apply

When proposing edits, scan the draft for:

- (a) academic or Latinate vocabulary that has a Germanic-root equivalent;
- (b) passive voice (`be` + past participle) and copular `was/is + to + verb` constructions;
- (c) sentences with more than one clause that could split;
- (d) paragraphs whose sentences do not visibly hand off to each other (missing transitions, abrupt topic jumps).

Flag and offer the simpler version.
